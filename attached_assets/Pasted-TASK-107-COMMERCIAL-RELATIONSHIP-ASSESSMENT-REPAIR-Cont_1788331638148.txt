5. COMMERCIAL ROLE SEMANTICS
==================================================

Commercial Role is SELLER/OFFERING RELATIVE.

It is NOT an intrinsic company property.

Correct conceptual input:

Candidate:
Company + business model + primary business + products/services

Seller:
Business Twin + specific offering

Then determine:

How does the candidate commercially relate to THIS seller offering?

Supported roles:

POTENTIAL_BUYER
SELLER_COMPETITOR
ADJACENT_VENDOR
PARTNER_POSSIBLE
UNKNOWN

==================================================
6. REQUIRED DECISION ORDER
==================================================

The Commercial Relationship Assessment should reason in this order:

STEP 1 — UNDERSTAND CANDIDATE

What does the candidate primarily sell or deliver?

Use evidence-backed Company Understanding.

Do not infer from industry alone.

--------------------------------------------------

STEP 2 — UNDERSTAND SELLER OFFERING

What specific commercial outcome/product/service does the seller offer?

Do not compare only company industries.

Compare actual offerings.

--------------------------------------------------

STEP 3 — DIRECT OFFERING OVERLAP

Ask:

Does the candidate sell substantially the same solution/outcome to substantially the same buyer?

If YES:

SELLER_COMPETITOR

This requires meaningful commercial offering overlap.

Shared industry alone is not enough.

Shared buyer persona alone is not enough.

Shared technology alone is not enough.

--------------------------------------------------

STEP 4 — COMPLEMENTARY / ADJACENT RELATIONSHIP

If candidate does NOT directly compete, ask:

Does it primarily sell a complementary product/service around the seller's offering such that the natural commercial relationship is vendor-to-vendor rather than buyer-to-seller?

If clearly complementary:

ADJACENT_VENDOR

But do not use ADJACENT_VENDOR merely because the candidate itself is a B2B vendor.

Most B2B vendors still purchase services from other vendors.

--------------------------------------------------

STEP 5 — PARTNERSHIP STRUCTURE

Ask:

Is there evidence of a natural reseller, referral, integration, delivery, channel, or joint-go-to-market relationship?

Only then:

PARTNER_POSSIBLE

Do not call something a partner simply because offerings are complementary.

--------------------------------------------------

STEP 6 — BUYER CAPABILITY

If the candidate does not directly compete and is structurally capable of consuming the seller's offering:

POTENTIAL_BUYER

A company may sell products/services itself and still be a buyer.

Examples generically:

A SaaS company can buy marketing services.
A consulting company can buy cybersecurity.
A cybersecurity software vendor can buy HR services.
A marketing agency can buy finance software.

Do not confuse:

"they sell something"

with:

"they cannot buy this offering."

--------------------------------------------------

STEP 7 — UNKNOWN

Use UNKNOWN only when:

- candidate business cannot be understood
- seller offering cannot be understood
- evidence materially conflicts
- relationship cannot reasonably be determined

UNKNOWN is not the fallback merely because the candidate has multiple business activities.

==================================================
7. BUYER-CAPABILITY TEST
==================================================

Introduce or strengthen an explicit generic question:

"Can this candidate reasonably consume this seller offering as an internal or external business service/product?"

This should be evaluated AFTER ruling out direct competition.

Conceptually:

direct competitor?
→ yes → SELLER_COMPETITOR

otherwise:

structurally capable of consuming seller offering?
→ yes → POTENTIAL_BUYER

unless there is stronger evidence for:

ADJACENT_VENDOR
or
PARTNER_POSSIBLE

This does NOT mean every non-competitor is automatically a buyer.

The assessment must still be evidence-based.

==================================================
8. SAME CUSTOMER ≠ COMPETITOR
==================================================

Explicitly prevent this failure:

Candidate and seller target similar customers
≠
candidate and seller compete.

Example generic logic:

Seller offers Service A to B2B SaaS companies.
Candidate offers Service B to B2B SaaS companies.

If A and B solve materially different problems:

do NOT classify as SELLER_COMPETITOR solely because customer segments overlap.

==================================================
9. SAME INDUSTRY ≠ COMPETITOR
==================================================

Explicitly prevent:

industry overlap
→ competitor

Examples:

two technology companies are not necessarily competitors.

two professional-services companies are not necessarily competitors.

two cybersecurity companies are not necessarily competitors if their actual offerings do not overlap materially.

Competition must be based primarily on:

commercial offering overlap.

==================================================
10. BEING A VENDOR ≠ ADJACENT_VENDOR
==================================================

This distinction is important.

Many candidates are themselves B2B vendors.

That alone must NOT cause:

ADJACENT_VENDOR.

ADJACENT_VENDOR should mean:

the candidate's commercial relationship to this specific seller offering is primarily complementary/vendor-side.

Not:

"the company sells services."

If the company can reasonably buy the seller's offering for itself:

POTENTIAL_BUYER may be the stronger relationship.

==================================================
11. PRIMARY VS SECONDARY OFFERINGS
==================================================

When candidates have several offerings:

weight primary commercial activity more strongly.

Do not classify a company as competitor because one minor feature overlaps with the seller.

Competition should require:

material overlap in primary offering
OR
meaningful revenue/product-line overlap
OR
clear substitutability from buyer perspective.

A small secondary capability should not automatically produce SELLER_COMPETITOR.

==================================================
12. SUBSTITUTABILITY TEST
==================================================

Add/strengthen a generic reasoning question:

"Would a reasonable buyer treat the candidate's offering and seller's offering as substitutes for the same purchasing decision?"

If YES:
competition evidence is strong.

If NO:
do not classify as SELLER_COMPETITOR merely because terminology overlaps.

This is an important generic distinction.
