PHASE 2A — BUSINESS TWIN MATURITY & STARTUP SUPPORT

JYRA
Who. When. Why.

IMPORTANT:

The Business Twin from Phase 2 is already implemented.

DO NOT rebuild Phase 2.
DO NOT remove existing Business Twin functionality.
DO NOT break existing Business Twin records.

This is an extension to make Business Twin work correctly for companies
at different stages, including startups with zero customers.

Read the existing Business Twin implementation, database schema,
versioning logic and documentation before making changes.

==================================================
PROBLEM
==================================================

The current Business Twin may assume that a company already has customers.

That assumption is incorrect.

JYRA must support:

- pre-launch companies
- startups with zero customers
- companies with pilot/design partners
- companies with a few early customers
- growing businesses
- mature businesses

JYRA must NEVER require a startup to fabricate:

best customers
bad customers
average deal size
win patterns
sales history
conversion patterns

Unknown information must remain unknown.

==================================================
1. BUSINESS MATURITY
==================================================

Add:

business_maturity_stage

Supported values:

PRE_LAUNCH
LAUNCHED_NO_CUSTOMERS
EARLY_CUSTOMERS
REPEATABLE_SALES
ESTABLISHED

Ask near the beginning:

"What stage best describes your business?"

Options:

Pre-launch / validating the idea

Launched, but no customers yet

Early customers / pilots

Growing with repeatable sales

Established business

==================================================
2. ADAPTIVE BUSINESS TWIN
==================================================

Business Twin questions must adapt to maturity.

Do NOT show irrelevant mandatory questions.

==================================================
3. PRE-LAUNCH / NO CUSTOMERS
==================================================

For:

PRE_LAUNCH
LAUNCHED_NO_CUSTOMERS

focus on:

What do you sell?

What problem does it solve?

Who experiences this problem?

How painful is the problem?

How do companies solve it today?

What alternatives exist?

Why is your solution different?

What events make the problem urgent?

Who do you believe would benefit most?

Which industries?

What company sizes?

Which geographies?

Who is likely to use the product?

Who is likely to champion it?

Who is likely to approve it?

Who likely controls the budget?

What would make a company NOT suitable?

Which 5–10 companies would you love to win as your first customers?

Why do you believe those companies would buy?

Have you spoken with prospective customers?

Do you have:

design partners
pilot users
beta users
waitlist users
LOIs
active prospects

What have those prospects told you?

What assumptions have actually been validated?

==================================================
4. EARLY CUSTOMERS
==================================================

For:

EARLY_CUSTOMERS

ask both hypothesis and evidence questions.

Examples:

Who are your current customers?

Which are paying customers?

Which are pilots?

Why did they start using the product?

What problem were they trying to solve?

Who initiated the conversation?

Who approved the purchase?

What happened immediately before they became interested?

Which customers appear to get the most value?

Which customers are poor fits?

But:

Do not infer strong patterns from tiny samples.

If there are only 2 customers, JYRA should not claim:

"Your ideal customer is..."

Instead say:

"Early evidence suggests..."

==================================================
6. EVIDENCE VS HYPOTHESIS
==================================================

This distinction is critical.

Business Twin information should support provenance such as:

FOUNDER_HYPOTHESIS
CUSTOMER_INTERVIEW
DESIGN_PARTNER
PILOT
CUSTOMER
CRM_HISTORY
SALES_OUTCOME
USER_CONFIRMED
AI_INFERRED

Do not treat all Business Twin statements as equally proven.

==================================================
7. VALIDATION STATUS
==================================================

Allow important Business Twin assumptions to be classified:

UNTESTED
PARTIALLY_VALIDATED
VALIDATED
CONTRADICTED
UNKNOWN

Example:

Target Industry:
B2B SaaS

Source:
FOUNDER_HYPOTHESIS

Status:
UNTESTED

Later:

6 qualified opportunities from B2B SaaS

Status may become:
PARTIALLY_VALIDATED

Do NOT automatically promote assumptions to VALIDATED based on weak evidence.

==================================================
8. ICP MODE
==================================================

When generating the ICP, classify it as:

HYPOTHESIS_ICP
EARLY_EVIDENCE_ICP
VALIDATED_ICP

Examples:

Startup with zero customers:

ICP MODE:
HYPOTHESIS

Message:

"This ICP is based primarily on your current market assumptions.
JYRA will help test and refine it as real outcomes accumulate."

Established company with substantial outcome history:

ICP MODE:
VALIDATED / EVIDENCE-LED

Do not fabricate a numerical confidence percentage unless the methodology
supports it.

==================================================
9. ICP ASSUMPTIONS
==================================================

For startups, explicitly store assumptions.

Examples:

"We believe companies with 100–500 employees experience this problem."

"We believe the CTO is the economic buyer."

"We believe international expansion creates urgency."

These should later be testable against outcomes.

==================================================
14. UX LANGUAGE
==================================================

Never make startup founders feel that their Business Twin is incomplete
simply because they have no customers.

Instead explain:

"No customers yet? That's fine. We'll start with your market hypothesis
and help you validate it as JYRA learns from real opportunities."

However, remain analytically honest.

Do not imply that founder assumptions have the same evidentiary value
as actual sales outcomes.
