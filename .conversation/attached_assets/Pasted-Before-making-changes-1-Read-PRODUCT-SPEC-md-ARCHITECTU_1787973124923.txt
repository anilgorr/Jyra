You are the principal product engineer and AI architect building a new B2B SaaS application called DigiSignal.

Build the application from scratch.

PRODUCT NAME

DigiSignal

PRODUCT PHILOSOPHY

Who. When. Why.

CORE PRODUCT PROMISE

DigiSignal continuously researches a seller's market, detects evidence of commercial need and timing, identifies the companies that deserve attention, explains why now, identifies relevant buyers, and learns from actual sales outcomes.

DigiSignal is NOT primarily:

- a lead database
- an email scraper
- a cold-email platform
- a CRM
- an outreach sequencer
- a generic AI research agent

The core product question is:

"Out of all possible companies, which small number should this seller focus on now, why, and what should they do next?"

CORE ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES

1. Company first, people later.

Do not discover and enrich thousands of contacts first.

Determine whether a company is commercially interesting before spending money finding people.

2. Evidence before interpretation.

The system must preserve original source evidence.

3. Separate:

FACT
SIGNAL
INTERPRETATION
HYPOTHESIS

Example:

FACT:
Company has seven open cybersecurity roles.

SIGNAL:
Security hiring acceleration.

INTERPRETATION:
Security investment appears to be increasing.

HYPOTHESIS:
The company may need managed security services.

Never store these as the same thing.

4. Every important commercial conclusion should be explainable.

5. Every Why Now statement must be traceable to evidence.

6. LLMs must not control:

- tenant authorization
- identity resolution
- scoring mathematics
- billing
- evidence provenance
- provider cost accounting
- database permissions
- canonical entity identity

7. Use AI where it adds value:

- Business Twin interpretation
- ICP assistance
- structured fact extraction
- ambiguous classification
- research-question generation
- Why Now writing
- explanation

8. Use deterministic logic wherever possible:

- scoring
- signal detection
- decay
- opportunity state
- confidence calculation
- research budgets
- provider routing
- stopping rules

9. Missing information is uncertainty.

Missing evidence must not automatically mean absence of need.

10. Optimize research cost.

Research cheap sources first.

Spend more only on companies where additional information could change the commercial decision.

11. Public company intelligence should be reusable globally.

Customer-specific commercial interpretations must remain private.

12. Avoid premature infrastructure.
